# Dialogue and lip sync requirements

## Three simultaneous limits

1. **Screenplay:** no dialogue line exceeds **10 words**; nobody speaks more than **two lines in a row**.
2. **Shot list:** no more than **one dialogue line per shot** and every shot is **3–6 seconds**. The spoken delivery must plausibly fit those seconds, including a breath before and after; if not, suggest a different approved cut or a shorter line rather than forcing it.
3. **Image/video prompt:** no more than **100 characters of spoken dialogue per shot**, including punctuation and spaces. This is a maximum, not a target. Quote and report the literal count. In silent shots use `DIALOGUE: NONE (0 characters)`.

Count spoken letters, spaces and punctuation inside the quote, excluding the surrounding quotation marks, descriptions and speaker labels. If more than one spoken line occurs due to a user-supplied approved exception, their combined count uses one joining space, but the standard here is **one line and one speaker per shot**.

## Speaker identification

Write the speaking person **by clothing colour, clothing type, frame side and key facial features immediately before the exact line**:

> ONLY SPEAKER: the older man in the DARK NAVY traditional tunic at FRAME RIGHT, short greying hair and the approved EDWARD face, speaking English with a deep Nigerian accent: "...".

> SILENT LISTENER: the young woman in the IVORY embroidered gown at FRAME LEFT, braided hair and the approved AMARA face. Her lips remain CLOSED; she does not speak, mouth, whisper or echo the line.

Maintain every established voice lock word for word between shots, varying only acting intensity. If a model assigns dialogue to the wrong character, regenerate a speaker-focused start image with a clear unobstructed mouth, and render again. A silent reaction cutaway often works better than an extended two-person speaking shot.

## Filmable action

AI video can animate a visible movement, not an internal thought. Use precise visible behaviour: eyes shift, chin lifts, hands tighten, door opens, cup is placed down. Avoid unexplained emotional labels or vague actions such as "realises his life is ruined". End screenplay scenes and shot lists on visual tension, not a final spoken line.
